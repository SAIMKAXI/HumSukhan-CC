# Routing around Android's locked call audio

*Research synthesis for HumSukhan. Compiled 2026-10-25 from six research notes (Android platform, OEM/Google system features, acoustic and hardware bridges, carrier and network routes, streaming ASR for Urdu telephony, iOS). Evidence labels: **[Primary]** means read directly from a primary source (developer.android.com, AOSP source via GitHub mirrors, developer.apple.com, the ESP-IDF README, FCC/Jazz/Telnyx pages, Hugging Face model cards). **[Snippet]** means the primary URL is cited but its content came from a search-engine snippet because the site would not load in the research sandbox. **[Secondary]** means press, blogs or forums. **[Inference]** means our reasoning, not a sourced fact. Many primary sites failed to load during research (support.google.com, play.google.com, source.android.com, android.googlesource.com, samsung.com, apple.com/support.apple.com, deepgram.com, soniox.com, twilio.com, docomo.ne.jp, nllapps.com, cubeacr.app, docs.espressif.com, sipfront.com, hamiltoncaptel.com and others), so every [Snippet] fact must be re-verified on the live page before it drives a budget or a policy decision.*

## 1. Executive verdict

**No ordinary Play-installed app on an unrooted Android phone can obtain the other caller's audio during a SIM call in October 2026, and none of the user-grantable roles (default dialer included) changes that.** The downlink sources require `CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT` or `CALL_AUDIO_INTERCEPTION`, both `signature|privileged|role` permissions, and since Android 10 audioserver silences an ordinary app's microphone while the phone is in `MODE_IN_CALL` ([Android: Sharing audio input](https://developer.android.com/media/platform/sharing-audio-input); [AudioPolicyService.cpp, Android 16](https://github.com/LineageOS/android_frameworks_av/blob/lineage-23.0/services/audiopolicy/service/AudioPolicyService.cpp)). The single loophole, mic capture by an accessibility service, still yields only acoustic speakerphone leakage (often removed by echo cancellation), and Play policy since 11 May 2022 says the Accessibility API "cannot be requested for remote call audio recording" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/google-is-banning-third-party-call-recording-apps-from-the-play-store-093201443.html)). Built-in system captioners (Google Live Caption, Samsung Live Translate) are the only same-phone software that can hear the call, and **neither supports Urdu** ([Google Live Caption help, snippet](https://support.google.com/accessibility/answer/9350862?hl=EN-GB&ref_topic=9114916); [Samsung Newsroom, 2026-10-14, snippet](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-expands-language-support-across-ai-experiences-on-galaxy)). The requirement therefore cannot be met by an app alone. **The recommended near-term deliverable is a small Bluetooth hands-free (HFP) accessory, "Caption Link", that pairs with the phone like a car kit, receives the remote caller's voice digitally, and hands it to HumSukhan for on-device or cloud captioning.** It works with the stock dialer on any Bluetooth phone, the other caller changes nothing, it needs no privileged permission and no telecom licence, and it isolates the remote voice from the user's own. It is **conditionally practical**: Espressif ships an official ESP32 HFP hands-free example ([ESP-IDF hfp_hf README](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/examples/bluetooth/bluedroid/classic_bt/hfp_hf/README.md)), but nobody has published an end-to-end phone-to-app captioning build, so a one-to-two-week hardware proof must pass before any call-caption UI is built. The software-only fallback is a conference merge with a HumSukhan cloud number (Ava's model on iPhone), which works today but costs the user a second airtime leg (about **Rs 3.5/min on Jazz**) on every call ([Jazz conference call](https://jazz.com.pk/postpaid/conference-call)). The long-term objective, which would finally satisfy every requirement with no accessory, is an **OEM preload with Transsion (Infinix, Tecno, itel; about 44% of Pakistani unit sales)** of a privileged call-audio bridge holding `CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT` ([Profit/Pakistan Today, 2025-10-20](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/10/20/xiaomi-eyes-pakistans-expanding-smartphone-market-as-local-assembly-and-smart-tv-production-gain-pace/)).

## 2. Verified explanation of the technical obstacle

### The downlink is a privileged audio source, enforced in native code

Android documents three audio sources that carry call audio: `VOICE_CALL` (uplink plus downlink), `VOICE_DOWNLINK` (the remote caller, Rx) and `VOICE_UPLINK` (Tx). Each reference entry says capture "requires the Manifest.permission.CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT permission. This permission is reserved for use by system components and is not available to third-party applications" ([MediaRecorder.AudioSource, updated 2026-08-03](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaRecorder.AudioSource)) [Primary]. The permission reference repeats "Not for use by third-party applications" ([Manifest.permission, updated 2026-10-09](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/Manifest.permission)) [Primary]. In the AOSP framework manifest, **`CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT`, `CAPTURE_MEDIA_OUTPUT`, `CAPTURE_VOICE_COMMUNICATION_OUTPUT`, `CALL_AUDIO_INTERCEPTION` and `CONTROL_INCALL_EXPERIENCE` are all `signature|privileged|role`** ([AOSP AndroidManifest.xml, GitHub mirror, main](https://github.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/blob/main/core/res/AndroidManifest.xml)) [Primary; mirror currency unverified]. The check is not cosmetic: in Android 16's `AudioPolicyService::AudioPolicyClient::checkPermissionForInput`, the three voice sources are tested first for `CALL_AUDIO_INTERCEPTION` and then for `CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT`, and anything else is denied ([AudioPolicyInterfaceImpl.cpp, LineageOS 23.0](https://github.com/LineageOS/android_frameworks_av/blob/lineage-23.0/services/audiopolicy/service/AudioPolicyInterfaceImpl.cpp)) [Primary].

### The "role" escape hatch is closed to user-installed apps

The `role` protection flag sounds like a door, but only hidden system roles satisfy it. On Android 16's PermissionController, `SYSTEM_AUDIO_INTELLIGENCE` and `SYSTEM_AMBIENT_AUDIO_INTELLIGENCE` hold the capture permissions, and `SYSTEM_CALL_STREAMING` (Android 14+) grants `CALL_AUDIO_INTERCEPTION`. **All three are `systemOnly="true"`, `visible="false"`, `static="true"`**. The user-grantable `ROLE_DIALER` grants phone, contacts, SMS, microphone, camera and notification permission sets plus `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW`, and **no capture permission** ([roles.xml, LineageOS 23.0](https://github.com/LineageOS/android_packages_modules_Permission/blob/lineage-23.0/PermissionController/res/xml/roles.xml)) [Primary]. The Telecom-side interception APIs (`isPstnCallAudioInterceptable`, `getCallDownlinkExtractionAudioRecord`, `getCallUplinkInjectionAudioTrack`) are `@hide @SystemApi` and require `CALL_AUDIO_INTERCEPTION` ([AudioManager.java, AOSP mirror](https://github.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/blob/main/media/java/android/media/AudioManager.java)). `Call.enterBackgroundAudioProcessing()` and `CallScreeningService`'s audio-processing option are also `@SystemApi` ([Call.java](https://github.com/LineageOS/android_frameworks_base/blob/lineage-23.0/telecomm/java/android/telecom/Call.java); [CallScreeningService.java](https://github.com/LineageOS/android_frameworks_base/blob/lineage-23.0/telecomm/java/android/telecom/CallScreeningService.java)) [Primary]. Becoming the default dialer gives HumSukhan control of the call UI, not the call's audio.

### Even the microphone goes silent during a cellular call

The fallback idea of putting the call on speaker and recording the phone's own mic fails on the same phone. Android's concurrent-capture rules say that while `getMode()` is `MODE_IN_CALL` or `MODE_IN_COMMUNICATION`, "the call always receives audio"; another app may capture only "if it is an accessibility service", or capture the call itself "if it is a privileged (pre-installed) app with permission CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT" ([Sharing audio input, updated 2026-10-16](https://developer.android.com/media/platform/sharing-audio-input)) [Primary]. The source code is explicit: `updateUidStates_l()` computes `isInCall = mPhoneState == AUDIO_MODE_IN_CALL` and lets a client capture only if `canBypassConcurrentPolicy`, which is true only for holders of `CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT` or of a new, privileged-only, feature-flagged `BYPASS_CONCURRENT_RECORD_AUDIO_RESTRICTION` ([AudioPolicyService.cpp, Android 16](https://github.com/LineageOS/android_frameworks_av/blob/lineage-23.0/services/audiopolicy/service/AudioPolicyService.cpp)). The same rule existed in Android 10 ([AudioPolicyService.cpp, Android 10](https://github.com/LineageOS/android_frameworks_av/blob/lineage-17.1/services/audiopolicy/service/AudioPolicyService.cpp)) [Primary]. An ordinary app's `AudioRecord` keeps running but receives silence, which `AudioRecordingConfiguration.isClientSilenced()` reports [Primary doc; on-device behaviour untested, Inference]. Google's own Live Transcribe hits the same wall: users report it says the microphone is in use during calls, even on speaker ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1479092/how-to-use-live-transcribe-android/)) [Secondary].

### Playback capture cannot see a modem call

`AudioPlaybackCapture` (MediaProjection, Android 10+) captures only playback whose usage is `USAGE_MEDIA`, `USAGE_GAME` or `USAGE_UNKNOWN`: "All other usages CAN NOT be captured" ([AudioPlaybackCaptureConfiguration, updated 2026-08-03](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/AudioPlaybackCaptureConfiguration)) [Primary]. A CS or modem-handled VoLTE call is not app playback at all; it runs in the modem/DSP path to `TYPE_TELEPHONY` devices, so there is nothing for MediaProjection to capture [Inference; no primary doc states this explicitly].

### The accessibility exception is real in code but blocked by policy and physics

Android 16 code lets an accessibility service capture with `VOICE_RECOGNITION` or `HOTWORD` while a call is active if any accessibility UID is "on top" (process state TOP through BOUND_FOREGROUND_SERVICE) ([AudioPolicyService.cpp, Android 16](https://github.com/LineageOS/android_frameworks_av/blob/lineage-23.0/services/audiopolicy/service/AudioPolicyService.cpp)) [Primary]. That is how Cube ACR and NLL's recorders kept working, through accessibility "connectors" and sideloaded helpers ([Cube ACR FAQ, snippet](https://cubeacr.app/faq.html); [NLL: Future of ACR, snippet](https://nllapps.com/apps/acr/future)). Two problems make it unusable for HumSukhan. First, **Play's Accessibility API policy, enforced from 11 May 2022, says the API "is not designed and cannot be requested for remote call audio recording"**, and Google described "remote" as recording where the other party is unaware ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/google-is-banning-third-party-call-recording-apps-from-the-play-store-093201443.html); [The Register, 2022-04-22](https://www.theregister.com/2022/04/22/google_banning_thirdparty_callrecording_apps/)) [Secondary reporting of policy text; the live policy page could not be loaded]. HumSukhan's live captioning is not storage-based recording, but an accessibility service that streams an unaware party's voice is squarely in the reviewer's risk zone, and no Google statement says live captioning is exempt. Second, the audio is still only the mic: the remote voice arrives acoustically from the loudspeaker, and NLL names the phone's echo canceller as the thing that strips it out, advising speakerphone and high gain ([NLL Android 9 page, snippet](https://nllapps.com/apps/acr/android9.htm)). A GrapheneOS user recorded only their own voice unless on speaker ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/2594-call-recording/29)) [Secondary].

### The platform's direction is more lock-down, not less

Nothing in Android 14 to 16 opens call audio to third parties. The new capabilities (system call streaming, audio-intelligence roles, the concurrent-record bypass) are all system-only, and foreground-service rules tightened: a `microphone` FGS cannot start from the background or from `BOOT_COMPLETED` ([FGS types, updated 2026-10-23](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types); [Background-start restrictions, updated 2026-10-23](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)) [Primary]. Android 17 code was not read, and the Android 16/17 behaviour-change pages were not mined for audio items, so a late change is possible but no search suggested one [Gap]. iOS is stricter still: CallKit and `CXCallObserver` expose call state only, an incoming call interrupts the app's `AVAudioSession`, and Apple's only reply to a developer asking to keep recording during rings was to file an enhancement request (FB18434531, May 2025) ([CXCallObserver](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxcallobserver); [Handling audio interruptions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/handling-audio-interruptions); [Apple Developer Forums 784782](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/784782)) [Primary].

## 3. Research findings and primary-source citations

### Who can legitimately hear a SIM call today

The audio-policy evidence produces a short list of parties that can lawfully receive the remote caller's voice: the OS vendor's own captioner, a privileged pre-installed app, a Bluetooth or wired accessory the phone routes audio to, and the telephone network. Everything HumSukhan can do maps to one of those four. **Privileged pre-installed apps are the only sanctioned in-phone route**: Android's own documentation says such an app "can capture the voice call" by choosing `VOICE_UPLINK`, `VOICE_DOWNLINK` or `TYPE_TELEPHONY` ([Sharing audio input](https://developer.android.com/media/platform/sharing-audio-input)) [Primary], and OEM call recorders such as MediaTek's `com.mediatek.callrecorder` ship exactly this way ([Bayton sysapps](https://sysapps.bayton.org/packages/com.mediatek.callrecorder)) [Secondary]. The open-source recorder BCR demonstrates the mechanism: as a system app it gets `CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT` and `CONTROL_INCALL_EXPERIENCE` and records `VOICE_CALL`, including separate uplink and downlink channels on Pixels, but it requires root or custom firmware and explicitly lists "Support for stock, unrooted firmware" and mic-plus-speaker workarounds as non-features ([BCR README](https://github.com/chenxiaolong/BCR/blob/master/README.md)) [Primary]. Google said in 2022 that pre-loaded dialers are unaffected by the accessibility ban because "accessibility capability is not required to get access to the incoming audio stream" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/google-is-banning-third-party-call-recording-apps-from-the-play-store-093201443.html)) [Secondary]. No public OEM partner programme, price or precedent for preloading a third-party caption app was found; Rogervoice's Android manifest declares `MANAGE_OWN_CALLS` and `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_PHONE_CALL` but not `CALL_AUDIO_INTERCEPTION`, which suggests it captions calls it carries itself ([AppBrain, Rogervoice](https://www.appbrain.com/app/rogervoice-phone-call-captions/com.rogervoice.app)) [Secondary].

### System captioners exist but skip Urdu, and most Pakistani phones lack them

Google Live Caption captions phone calls on-device on "Pixel and selected Android phones", with type-to-reply; its Android 14+ language list of about 15 languages includes Hindi but **not Urdu** ([Live Caption help, snippet](https://support.google.com/accessibility/answer/9350862?hl=EN-GB&ref_topic=9114916); [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/how-to-use-android-live-caption/)) [Snippet]. A March 2026 guide says most non-Pixel phones do not reliably support call captions ([MacMyths](https://macmyths.com/how-to-use-live-caption-to-transcribe-phone-calls/)) [Secondary]. Pixel Call Notes is limited to a handful of countries that exclude Pakistan ([Google Phone app help](https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/15257579?hl=en)) [Snippet]. Samsung's Galaxy AI reached **24 languages on 2026-10-14, again with Hindi and Gujarati but no Urdu and no English (Pakistan)** ([Samsung Global Newsroom, snippet](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-expands-language-support-across-ai-experiences-on-galaxy)), and call transcripts are absent from Galaxy A56, A36, A26 and A17 5G ([Samsung India support](https://www.samsung.com/in/support/mobile-devices/how-to-use-call-transcript-feature-in-galaxy-devices/)) [Snippet]. OEM call-AI features on Honor (6 languages), OPPO, Infinix Folax (auto-answer English-only in one review) and Xiaomi list no Urdu ([GSMArena, Honor Magic V5](https://www.gsmarena.com/honor_magic_v5_has_ondevice_ai_realtime_call_translation-news-69193.php); [91mobiles](https://www.91mobiles.com/reviews/infinix-ai-features-reviewed-pros-cons/)) [Secondary]. The market reality sharpens the gap: Transsion held **44%** of Pakistani units in Q2 2025, followed by Xiaomi 18%, vivo 17%, Samsung 10% and OPPO 6% ([Profit/Pakistan Today](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/10/20/xiaomi-eyes-pakistans-expanding-smartphone-market-as-local-assembly-and-smart-tv-production-gain-pace/)), and 93% of phones sold in 2025 were locally assembled, led by Infinix (3.65M units) ([MEA Tech Watch, PTA data](https://meatechwatch.com/2026/01/22/local-mobile-phone-manufacturing-dipped-in-december-2025-pta/)) [Secondary]. No source shows an API to read Live Caption text; reading its overlay through an accessibility service is undocumented, untested, and scrapes another app's UI ([Create an accessibility service](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/service)) [Primary doc; feasibility Inference].

### Bluetooth hands-free is the cleanest accessory path, and the parts exist

In Bluetooth HFP the phone is the Audio Gateway and a headset or car kit is the Hands-Free unit; the call's downlink travels to the HF unit as digital audio over SCO, **CVSD at 8 kHz or mSBC at 16 kHz**, with no echo cancellation applied against it [Inference from the role model in [BlueALSA wiki](https://github.com/arkq/bluez-alsa/wiki/Using-BlueALSA-with-HFP-and-HSP-Devices)]. Espressif's official `hfp_hf` example, read directly, targets the classic **ESP32** (e.g. ESP32-DevKitC) and ESP32-S31, connects "to an AG running on a smartphone", answers and rejects calls, and offers a **vHCI data path that routes audio "through the Bluedroid to the Application layer"**, with mSBC decoded in software when wideband speech is negotiated ([ESP-IDF hfp_hf README](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/examples/bluetooth/bluedroid/classic_bt/hfp_hf/README.md)) [Primary]. The API exposes `esp_hf_client_connect_audio` and an `ESP_HF_AUDIO_STATE_CONNECTED_MSBC` state ([ESP-IDF v4.4 HFP Client API, snippet](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4.8/esp32/api-reference/bluetooth/esp_hf_client.html)). Linux can play the same role via BlueZ plus oFono or BlueALSA, with known pitfalls such as SCO rejected when no audio agent is registered ([lore.kernel.org](https://lore.kernel.org/all/5745BD55.3030108@gmail.com/T/); [PipeWire #2531](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/pipewire/pipewire/-/issues/2531)) [Secondary], and Sipfront published a March 2025 article on automating calls on real phones with a Raspberry Pi as HFP hands-free ([Sipfront blog, title/snippet only](https://sipfront.com/blog/2025/03/automating-calls-on-real-mobile-devices-with-bluetooth-hfp-on-a-raspberry-pi/)). Consumer precedent exists: Bluewire (2015) and the KJB D1450 (about USD 137) recorded both sides of mobile calls as Bluetooth hands-free units ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/01/27/the-bluewire-is-a-bluetooth-headset-that-can-record-your-calls/amp/); [123SecurityProducts](https://www.123securityproducts.com/audio/recording-devices/audio-standard/d1450.html)) [Secondary]. A second stock Android phone cannot be the HF unit, because `BluetoothHeadsetClient` is a hidden/System API ([AICP mirror](https://git.aicp-rom.com/packages_modules_Bluetooth.git/plain/framework/java/android/bluetooth/BluetoothHeadsetClient.java)) [Primary source mirror]. **What is missing is any published end-to-end build in which an ESP32 HF unit delivers a real phone's downlink PCM to app code**; the Espressif data-callback name was not confirmed because its docs site would not load [Gap]. Wired taps such as the JK Audio CellTap 4C (caller on the right channel, about USD 113) and hearing-aid protocols (ASHA, LE Audio HAP with LC3) are proven or specified but less practical: jackless phones need USB-C DACs, and no open-source ASHA or LE Audio telephony sink was found ([SCMS CellTap 4C](https://www.scmsinc.com/jk-audio-celltap4c-celltap-4c-wireless-phone-audio-tap-adapter.html); [Android Developers Blog, ASHA 2018](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2018/08/streaming-support-spec-for-hearing-aids_16.html); [BLE Audio overview](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/bluetooth/ble-audio/overview?hl=de)) [Primary/Secondary].

### Every commercial call-caption service owns the call path

US captioned-call products all avoid on-phone capture by putting the provider in the call path: CapTel and CaptionCall hardware phones, ClearCaptions and InnoCaption via **forwarding the user's mobile number to the provider's number**, and Nagish (now Rylo) via an app dialer that keeps the user's number; "the person on the other end doesn't need the app installed" ([ClearCaptions FAQ](https://clearcaptions.com/blog/faq-items/how-do-i-forward-my-mobile-number-to-my-clearcaptions-number); [InnoCaption support](https://innocaption.com/support); [Rylo blog](https://rylo.com/post/innocaption-vs-captioncall)) [Primary/Secondary]. The economics rest on regulation: the US TRS Fund pays **$1.05/min (FY2025-26) and $0.95/min (FY2026-27) for ASR-only IP CTS** ([FCC DA 25-571](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-571A1.pdf); [FCC DA 26-516, search-summary figures](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-516A1.pdf)), and France has obliged operators to fund accessible telephony since 8 October 2018, choosing Rogervoice ([service-public.fr](https://service-public.fr/particuliers/actualites/A12949?lang=en); [Siècle Digital](https://siecledigital.fr/2018/10/09/grace-a-rogervoice-les-sourds-et-malentendants-peuvent-passer-des-appels/)). On iPhone, Ava captions incoming calls by having the user accept a call from Ava, hold the original, and merge, which is a conference bridge ([Ava Help](https://help.ava.me/en/articles/5884530-captioning-incoming-phone-calls-with-ava)) [Primary]. The closest carrier precedent, NTT Docomo's **Mieru Denwa**, ran network-side speech recognition so the other party needed no app from February 2019 until it **closed on 29 March 2024**, with Docomo pointing users to Pixel captions and relay services ([Appllio, Feb 2019](https://appllio.com/20190221-10962-docomo-mieru-call); [Docomo release 2023-09-25, snippet](https://www.docomo.ne.jp/info/news_release/2023/09/25_00.html); [iPhone Mania](https://iphone-mania.jp/news-240007/amp/)) [Secondary/Snippet]. The closure reason was not found.

### Pakistan's network supports a merge, but numbers and law are the friction

Jazz lets prepaid users "add up to 4 numbers on a conference call", and "each call log will be treated and charged as a new call" ([Jazz conference call](https://jazz.com.pk/postpaid/conference-call)) [Primary], at roughly **Rs 3.50/min plus Rs 0.15 setup** on most packages ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/pakistans-prepaid-mobile-tariffs-put-users-under-growing-cost-pressure/)) [Secondary]. Ufone's conference data is from 2013 and excluded some packages; Zong's terms were not found; Telenor Pakistan was amalgamated into PTML (Ufone) on 1 July 2026 with billing migration pending ([ProPakistani 2013](https://propakistani.pk/2013/01/31/how-to-make-conference-calls/); [ProPakistani, 2026-07-02](https://propakistani.pk/2026/07/02/telenor-pakistan-formally-amalgamates-into-ptml-following-statutory-approvals/)). Telnyx sells Pakistani numbers against a letter of intent and recent proof of address and says local calls must route through in-country carriers ([Telnyx Pakistan DID requirements](https://support.telnyx.com/en/articles/11843465-pakistan-did-requirements); [Telnyx PSTN local calling](https://support.telnyx.com/en/articles/6622229-pstn-local-calling-with-telnyx)), while AVOXI notes only PTA-licensed LDI operators may terminate international calls into Pakistan ([AVOXI](https://www.avoxi.com/local-phone-number-did-ddi/virtual-phone-number-pakistan/)) and PTA/FIA routinely raid grey VoIP gateways ([ProPakistani 2011](https://propakistani.pk/2011/02/22/illegal-international-exchanges-busted-in-peshawar-and-karachi/)) [Secondary]. Media streaming is cheap, **$0.0035/min on Telnyx and about $0.0044/min on Twilio** ([Telnyx release note](https://telnyx.com/release-notes/media-streaming-and-decrypted-forking-service-pricing-updates); [Superdupr, 2026-10-07](https://superdupr.com/blog/twilio-pricing)) [Primary/Secondary], and Twilio's `<Stream>` can deliver separate inbound and outbound tracks ([Twilio Stream docs, snippet](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/stream)). Legally, Pakistan has **no enacted data-protection law as of mid-2026** ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=PK)); a single low-authority site claims all-party consent is required for call recording under PECA s.19 and a March 2026 Supreme Court ruling, which could not be corroborated ([RecordingLaw Pakistan](https://www.recordinglaw.com/world-laws/world-recording-laws/pakistan-recording-laws/)). No Pakistani relay fund, operator accessibility mandate or RTT deployment was found; the visible accessibility activity is PTA's DeafTawk MoU (January 2025) and Zong's AI sign-language service with ConnectHear (September 2026) ([PTA press release](https://pta.gov.pk/category/pta-signs-mou-with-deaftawk-to-advance-accessibility-for-the-special--need-hearing-impairments-867832596-2025-01-03); [Digital Pakistan](https://digitalpakistan.pk/zong-connecthear-launch-ai-sign-language-service/)).

### Urdu streaming ASR exists in the cloud and, barely, on-device

**Deepgram Nova-3 added monolingual Urdu (`ur`) on 2026-02-03 and improved it on 2026-08-17**, but its `language=multi` code-switching set of 10 languages includes Hindi and excludes Urdu, which is why HumSukhan runs parallel en-US and ur sockets ([Deepgram changelog 2026-02-03, snippet](https://developers.deepgram.com/changelog/2026/2/3); [changelog 2026-08-17, snippet](https://developers.deepgram.com/changelog/2026/8/17); [changelog 2025-03-31, snippet](https://developers.deepgram.com/changelog/2025/3/31)). **Soniox real-time claims native Urdu-English mid-sentence switching at $0.12/hour** and markets a 6.3% Urdu WER against Azure's 19.3%, a vendor claim on an undisclosed test set ([Soniox Urdu page, snippet](https://soniox.com/speech-to-text/urdu); [Soniox vs Azure, snippet](https://soniox.com/compare/soniox-vs-azure/urdu)). AssemblyAI's Universal-3.6 Pro Streaming adds Urdu with default code-switching, price unconfirmed ([AssemblyAI models, snippet](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/getting-started/models)). No vendor publishes Urdu WER on narrowband telephone audio. Open-model evidence is sobering: Whisper turbo scores 21.9% WER on FLEURS Urdu and 26.5% on Common Voice 15 ([openai/whisper #2363, snippet](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)), fine-tuned Whisper-large about 22–24% on read and conversational Urdu ([COLING 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.397/)), and the UrduSpeech corpus (89.4 h code-switched) found Whisper transliterating or translating English into Urdu script ([HF paper 2605.17846](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.17846)) [Primary via HF connector]. On-device, the community **Urdu-Streaming-Zipformer-20M for sherpa-onnx is 42 MiB int8, peaks at 133 MiB RAM, emits every 320 ms, and scores 30.49% macro WER, 37.71% on noisy and 46.93% on code-switched speech**, takes 16 kHz only, and states that telephone audio degrades it ([HF model card, updated 2026-10-25](https://huggingface.co/mahwizzzz/Urdu-Streaming-Zipformer-20M)) [Primary].

## 5. The single recommended solution

### Caption Link: a Bluetooth hands-free accessory feeding HumSukhan

**No solution satisfies every requirement for an ordinary third-party app; we say so explicitly.** The most practical route that does satisfy "the other caller changes nothing" on an ordinary low-cost Pakistani phone is a HumSukhan-branded Bluetooth HFP hands-free accessory. The phone treats it like any headset or car kit: the stock dialer, SIM, number and call are untouched, and the remote caller hears nothing different. The accessory receives the remote caller's voice as digital SCO audio and passes 20 ms PCM frames to the HumSukhan app, which captions them through the existing speech pipeline. **Does it truly satisfy "other caller changes nothing"? Yes.** It does not satisfy "app only": the Deaf user must pair an accessory once and keep it with the phone, which we judge acceptable for a usable product and far better than the alternatives (a second phone on speaker, or paying for a second call leg every time).

We recommend it over the conference-merge fallback for four reasons drawn from the evidence. It delivers **only the downlink**, so captions show the caller's words without the user's own voice mixed in. It works with **zero per-call steps and zero per-minute cost** once paired. It allows **on-device ASR**, so it keeps working when a budget phone on a CS call has no concurrent data, and audio never leaves the phone. And it touches **no privileged API, no accessibility capture, and no telephony licence**, so the Play and PTA exposure is limited to ordinary hardware certification and data-safety disclosure. The weakness is equally clear: it is a hardware product that nobody has shown end-to-end, so it carries hardware, supply and support risk that a software team has not carried before.

### What it needs

The hardware is a classic **ESP32** module (BR/EDR is required; ESP32-S2/S3/C3 lack Classic Bluetooth, and the Espressif example supports only ESP32 and ESP32-S31) running ESP-IDF's Bluedroid HFP-HF with the vHCI data path, an I2S MEMS microphone for the user's own speech (the phone's mic is bypassed while audio is on the accessory), and either a small battery or a USB-C tether ([ESP-IDF hfp_hf README](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/examples/bluetooth/bluedroid/classic_bt/hfp_hf/README.md)). We propose two form factors, decided by the Gate 0 tests. The **tethered plug** sits in the phone's USB-C port, draws power from it, and sends PCM over a USB-serial bridge to the app through Android's USB host API; this removes Bluetooth/BLE radio coexistence and accessory charging from the problem [Inference]. The **wireless clip** sends compressed frames over BLE GATT, which is more convenient but must prove that eSCO and BLE can share the ESP32's single 2.4 GHz radio without dropouts [Inference; flagged as a known stress case in the notes]. On the phone, HumSukhan needs a Kotlin companion service (`connectedDevice` foreground-service type, not `microphone`), the BLE or USB permission flow, Bluetooth pairing via the system dialog, and an overlay or lock-screen caption surface. No server is required for on-device mode; Supabase stays as today for accounts and settings.

### What the team can build, and what lies outside its control

The existing Flutter + Kotlin team can build the companion service, the frame protocol, the call-audio port, the caption surface, the engine selection logic, the benchmark harness and the Supabase-side telemetry. It should **contract an embedded Bluetooth engineer** for the ESP-IDF firmware, eSCO/codec tuning and power management, and later a **local electronics manufacturing partner** for enclosures and assembly. Outside the team's control are each OEM's Bluetooth stack behaviour (codec negotiation, auto-routing to the accessory, call-state indicators), Bluetooth SIG qualification for a commercial product, PTA type approval for a radio device (requirement unverified), import duties on modules, and the user's habit of carrying the accessory.

### The Deaf user's experience

The user pairs Caption Link once, guided by HumSukhan. When a call rings, the phone shows the normal incoming-call screen and the call audio goes to Caption Link automatically, as with a car kit. HumSukhan, woken by the accessory's call-state message, raises a high-contrast caption surface over the dialer and on the lock screen, labelled "Caller", with interim words updating in place and committed lines in larger type. A hard-of-hearing user can still listen through an earphone jack or small speaker on the accessory. The user speaks into the accessory mic, or types and lets HumSukhan's TTS speak into the call through the accessory's uplink, giving **type-to-speak inside an ordinary SIM call without root**, a capability no Play app can offer today [Inference; requires firmware SCO uplink injection]. If the user taps "speaker" or "earpiece" in the dialer, audio leaves the accessory, and HumSukhan shows "Captions paused: call audio moved off Caption Link", with a one-line instruction to tap the Bluetooth route again.

### Main risks and the fallback

The decisive risks are that some popular phones negotiate only CVSD 8 kHz (acceptable for ASR, but lower quality), that auto-routing or call-state signalling misbehaves on some OEM dialers, that BLE throughput collapses under eSCO (solved by the tethered form), that Urdu and code-switched accuracy on narrowband audio is too low on-device (mitigated by cloud engines when data exists and by narrowband fine-tuning), and that unit cost or distribution in Pakistan makes the accessory unaffordable. If Gate 0 fails, the **fallback is the conference-merge cloud bridge** (row 14), which the team can prove in two to three days and which also serves iPhone users. The **interim, available this month**, is a "Phone call on speaker" preset of HumSukhan's existing face-to-face mode running on a second device, plus guidance to use Live Caption or Samsung call captions for English or Hindi calls on phones that have them.

### The long-term, partnership-dependent objective

The route that removes the accessory entirely is an OEM preload. **Pursue Transsion Pakistan (Infinix, Tecno, itel) first**: it is the largest vendor by units (44% in Q2 2025), it assembles locally, and it already ships call AI (Folax) without Urdu ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/10/20/xiaomi-eyes-pakistans-expanding-smartphone-market-as-local-assembly-and-smart-tv-production-gain-pace/); [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2025/08/29/folax-ai-infinix-personal-assistant/)). The ask is narrow and standard: preload a small, HumSukhan-signed "call-audio bridge" in `/system/priv-app` with `CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT` allowlisted, reading `VOICE_DOWNLINK` and exposing frames only to the HumSukhan app through a signature-protected bound service; the Play-updated HumSukhan app does the captioning. vivo and Xiaomi are the next targets; Samsung is lower priority given its 10% share and its own Galaxy AI roadmap. The carrier alternative is a Jazz or Zong caption VAS on VoLTE (Zong already partners with ConnectHear), but Docomo's closure of Mieru Denwa after five years shows that carrier economics are weak without a mandate, so treat it as advocacy alongside PTA's accessibility work rather than a plan.

## 6. Proposed audio-to-caption architecture

### Keep acquisition behind its own port

HumSukhan's speech stack (app repository, `docs/architecture.md` as of PR #121, October 2026) already separates where audio comes from and how it is recognized: `EverydaySpeechProvider` runs a recognizer plan from the pure function `sttPlan()` (phone recognizer, Deepgram live stream in `EverydayBilingualSttService`, cloud per-sentence `CloudChunkStt`, offline `SherpaSTTProvider`), emits `SpeechResultEvent`s with interim and final text, hands over on failure via `CaptionWatchdog`, and decides when a caption is complete with the rule-based `TurnTaking`, `SpeakerPauses` and `SentenceAssembler` in `turn_taking.dart`; `SpeechCapability` reports what the device can do. Call captioning should add one new audio port and reuse that recognition stack: a native **`CallAudioPort`** that emits `AudioFrame(pcm, sampleRateHz ∈ {8000, 16000}, origin ∈ {CVSD, mSBC, AMR, μ-law}, channel ∈ {REMOTE, LOCAL, MIXED}, captureTimeNs, seq)` and a state stream `NoCall | Ringing | Active(route) | AudioLost(reason) | Ended`. Today every recognizer opens the microphone itself (through `record`'s `AudioRecorder` or `RecorderPcmSource`), so the one structural change is to let the cloud stream and the sherpa-onnx recognizer accept PCM frames pushed from a `CallAudioPort` instead. Implementations are `HfpBridgeSource` (USB-serial or BLE, the recommended route), `AcousticMicSource` (today's face-to-face mic, used for the second-device interim), `ConferenceBridgeSource` (fallback; audio lives on the server, so on the phone it is a caption-event source only), and a future `PrivilegedDownlinkSource` that wraps `AudioRecord(VOICE_DOWNLINK)` on an OEM-preload build. Each source reports whether it is available, alongside `SpeechCapability`, so the UI never offers call captions when the source cannot deliver audio. `AudioLost` and `Ended` are first-class states the UI renders, following the repository's rule that every stream terminus is handled.

### Native ownership and the Flutter boundary

Audio should never cross the platform channel. Kotlin owns acquisition, framing, VAD, resampling and the ASR adapters, and Flutter receives only caption events (`utteranceId, revision, isFinal, text, languageTag, speaker, audioStartMs, audioEndMs`) over an `EventChannel` or Pigeon-generated stream, plus source state. This avoids copying 16–32 KB/s of PCM into Dart, keeps captioning alive when the Flutter engine is backgrounded, and lets the same native pipeline feed a lock-screen caption activity. The accessory protocol should use 20 ms frames (160 samples at 8 kHz, 320 at 16 kHz) with sequence numbers and a checksum; the USB-serial path can carry raw 16-bit PCM (128–256 kbit/s), while the BLE path should carry 4:1 ADPCM or μ-law to stay well under practical BLE throughput [Inference].

### Engine choice for Urdu, English and code-switching on narrowband audio

No engine has published Urdu telephone WER, so the choice must come from HumSukhan's own benchmark (Stage 2). The working hypothesis is a three-tier policy. **On-device default:** sherpa-onnx `OnlineRecognizer` with Urdu-Streaming-Zipformer-20M, which fits 2–4 GB phones (42 MiB, 133 MiB peak RAM) and has Latin BPE tokens and some code-switched training data, but needs 8→16 kHz upsampling and narrowband fine-tuning (transcode training audio through AMR-NB at 8 kHz and back) before it is good enough on calls ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/mahwizzzz/Urdu-Streaming-Zipformer-20M)). **Cloud when data exists:** a single code-switching stream if Soniox (claimed native Urdu-English, $0.12/h) or AssemblyAI U-3.6 Pro beats the incumbent on the benchmark; otherwise the existing Deepgram en-US + ur dual-socket arbitration, sent at **8 kHz natively** because Deepgram accepts 8 kHz linear16 and the channel carries nothing above 4 kHz anyway ([Deepgram sample-rate doc, snippet](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/sample-rate)). **Never real-time Whisper on low-end phones**: segment-level Whisper gives final-only captions 1–3 s late and is too heavy for 2 GB devices [Inference]. Scoring must normalise script, because an English word in Latin or Urdu script would otherwise count as an error, a confound the UrduSpeech and COLING work both stress ([HF paper 2605.17846](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.17846); [COLING 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.397/)).

### Interim and final handling without duplication

Captions are keyed by `utteranceId` and `revision`. A partial replaces the current draft in place and never appends; a final commits the draft and closes the id. In dual-socket mode both sockets' events carry audio-time windows, and the arbiter holds each socket's final for a short window (for example 300 ms), commits exactly one winner per overlapping window using the existing arbitration in `EverydayBilingualSttService` (confidence, Urdu function-word plausibility, language continuity, and dropping the slower recognizer's copy of an utterance already shown), and discards a late loser whose window overlaps a committed one by more than half. The rule-based `TurnTaking` policy keeps its job: a pause commits a caption but does not end the session, and only the source's `Ended` state (call over) ends it. Professional-mode's "never flicker" rule extends to calls by showing interim drafts in a lighter style and committed lines in full weight.

### VAD, endpointing, resampling, bitrate and data

Silero VAD runs on the native side at 8 or 16 kHz with 32 ms frames (256 or 512 samples) at about 1 ms per frame ([Pipecat Silero VAD](https://docs.pipecat.ai/server/utilities/audio/silero-vad-analyzer)); it gates cloud uploads with a 300–500 ms hangover and a keep-alive during silence, which cuts both cost and data. Endpointing uses the engine's own control where available (Soniox `max_endpoint_delay_ms`, example 500 ms ([Soniox RT docs, snippet](https://soniox.com/docs/stt/rt/real-time-transcription))) and HumSukhan's `TurnTaking` rules otherwise. The accessory's downlink needs no echo cancellation or noise suppression, only DC removal and level normalisation; resampling happens once, 8→16 kHz polyphase, and only for 16 kHz-only engines. For cloud mode, the wire format should default to Opus at 16 kbps (about 7 MB/h) or μ-law (about 29 MB/h), not 16 kHz PCM (about 115 MB/h per socket), and one code-switching stream should replace two sockets wherever the benchmark allows. Because Android cannot know whether the user has a bundle, the app should ask once and default users without a bundle to on-device mode.

### Battery, low-end phones, locked screen, backgrounding and route changes

Because the accessory route captures no microphone on the phone, the companion service runs as a `connectedDevice` foreground service and avoids the while-in-use `RECORD_AUDIO` restrictions that block background microphone services ([FGS types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)). Associating the accessory through `CompanionDeviceManager` should let the system wake HumSukhan when Caption Link connects and permit starting the service from the background; this exemption and the use of `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` for an overlay over the dialer must be verified against the current restrictions page [Inference]. Captions on a locked screen use a caption activity with show-when-locked behaviour, with the latest line mirrored in the ongoing notification as a fallback. On low-end phones the single-thread Zipformer is estimated at about 20% of one core [Inference, unmeasured]; Stage 4 must measure battery drain and thermal throttling per hour of captioned call on 2–3 GB devices. Route changes are surfaced, not hidden: SCO disconnect or a user switch to speaker or earpiece moves the source to `AudioLost(routeChanged)`; call waiting and hold are tracked from HFP call-state indicators so captions are segmented per call; and a Bluetooth link loss triggers `Reconnecting` with a visible timer, matching the existing state machine.

## 7. Staged proof-of-concept and implementation roadmap

### Stage 1: prove or kill live remote audio (1–2 weeks)

The smallest decisive experiment is to flash Espressif's `hfp_hf` example with the vHCI data path onto two or three classic ESP32 DevKitC boards, pair each with three Pakistani phones from different OEM families (one Infinix or Tecno, one Samsung Galaxy A, one Redmi or vivo), place and receive real SIM calls on Jazz and Zong, and dump the received downlink PCM over UART to a laptop, where it is listened to and run through the existing Deepgram adapter. Success means intelligible remote speech with none of the user's own voice, on calls the user answered from the normal dialer. In the same week, two cheap side experiments close the other doors with evidence: a half-day ordinary-app test that logs `isClientSilenced()` and RMS during SIM calls on the same phones (expected: silence), and a two-to-three-day conference-merge test with a Telnyx Pakistani number (or any CPaaS number, if PK paperwork is slow) streaming its inbound track to Deepgram, to qualify the fallback. Specialist need: an embedded engineer familiar with ESP-IDF Bluedroid; total effort about 8–12 engineer-days [estimate].

### Stage 2: streaming-caption benchmark on consented call-like audio (2–3 weeks)

Build a 1–2 hour evaluation set in three slices: Urdu, Pakistani English and Urdu-English code-switching, from UrduSpeech and FLEURS clips transcoded through AMR-NB and CVSD at 8 kHz, plus 30–60 minutes of real calls recorded through the Stage 1 accessory with written consent from both speakers. Score Deepgram dual-socket, Soniox real-time, AssemblyAI U-3.6 Pro Streaming and the on-device Zipformer-20M with script-normalised WER, first-partial latency, final latency and duplication rate. A narrowband fine-tune of the Zipformer (4–6 weeks of ML work, CC-BY-4.0 attribution to AI4Bharat and ASLP-lab) is a follow-on, not a blocker. Specialists: an ASR/ML engineer and Urdu-literate annotators.

### Stage 3: integration prototype in HumSukhan's architecture (3–5 weeks)

Implement `CallAudioPort` and `HfpBridgeSource` (USB-serial first, BLE second), the frame protocol, VAD gating and resampling in Kotlin; let `EverydayBilingualSttService` and `SherpaSTTProvider` take pushed frames and route `CallAudioPort` frames into them; add the caption event contract, the over-dialer and lock-screen caption surfaces, the companion association, and the `AudioLost`/`Reconnecting` states in Flutter. Firmware work in parallel adds call-state messages, SCO uplink from the accessory mic, and TTS injection for type-to-speak.

### Stage 4: device and OS compatibility (2–3 weeks)

Test on 12–15 phones covering the Pakistani market by brand weight: Infinix (Hot, Smart, Note), Tecno (Spark, Pop, Camon), itel (A and P series), vivo Y series, Xiaomi Redmi A and Note series, Samsung Galaxy A0x/A1x, OPPO A series and realme C series, on Android 11 through 16 including Android Go builds, choosing exact models from current Pakistani best-seller lists. Record codec negotiated, auto-routing on incoming and outgoing calls, call-state signalling, dropouts, battery drain per hour and RAM headroom.

### Stage 5: latency measurement (1 week)

Measure capture to first partial to final with a controlled source: a test phone plays a script with embedded timing tones into the call, the accessory timestamps the frame at SCO receipt, and the app logs first-partial and final times against the tone onset. Report p50 and p95 separately for on-device and cloud, over Wi-Fi, 4G and 3G.

### Stage 6: condition testing (2–3 weeks)

Run the benchmark matrix across Urdu, English and mixed speech; street, market and generator noise at the caller's end; interruptions, overlapping speech, call waiting and hold; Bluetooth range and reconnects; a user switching to speakerphone mid-call; weak and absent data (forcing on-device fallback); and VoLTE versus CS calls on each operator. Include Deaf and hard-of-hearing testers through a community partner such as ConnectHear or DeafTawk, both already working with PTA or Zong.

### Stage 7: privacy, security, consent, retention, background execution and distribution (parallel, 2–4 weeks)

Default to no audio retention and transcript retention only on explicit user choice, using HumSukhan's existing retention policies; offer an optional spoken notice that the user is reading captions, injected through the accessory uplink, which reduces legal exposure without requiring the caller to do anything. Obtain Pakistani legal advice on PECA and the unverified 2026 consent ruling, complete Play Data safety disclosures for audio processing and any cloud processor (Deepgram with `mip_opt_out=true`, which keeps audio "only long enough to complete the request" ([Deepgram MIP doc, snippet](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/the-deepgram-model-improvement-partnership-program))), confirm the foreground-service declaration for `connectedDevice`, and scope Bluetooth SIG qualification and PTA type approval for the accessory. Specialists: a Pakistani telecom and privacy lawyer, and a certification consultant. Overall, Stages 1 to 7 take about **4–6 months to a field pilot** with a team of two app engineers, one contracted embedded engineer and part-time ML and legal support [estimate].

### Latency, risks and operating constraints

Estimated first-partial latency is 0.4–0.7 s on-device (320 ms Zipformer chunks plus compute) and 0.3–0.65 s for cloud engines (frame, uplink, engine partial, render), plus tens of milliseconds for accessory transport and an estimated 100–300 ms for the conference bridge; all are engineering estimates with no Pakistani field measurement, which Stage 5 must supply ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/mahwizzzz/Urdu-Streaming-Zipformer-20M)). Accuracy is the larger risk: on-device Urdu is about 30% WER on clean test sets and about 47% on code-switched speech before any telephone degradation, while cloud vendors' Urdu claims are self-published. Operationally, the accessory must be charged or tethered, carried, and supported; cloud modes need concurrent data during calls, which CS calls on 2G may not provide [Inference, device test]; the conference fallback depends on operator package rules and a legitimately sourced Pakistani number; and any route that sends a caller's voice to a foreign cloud processor carries consent exposure in a country with no data-protection law but uncertain recording-consent case law.

## 9. Explicit go/no-go decision criteria

**Gate 0, live remote audio (end of Stage 1). Go** if, on at least 3 of 3 initial phones from different OEM families, the accessory receives intelligible downlink audio for at least 99% of the duration of five 10-minute SIM calls each, with the call routed to it automatically on both incoming and outgoing calls and no user action beyond answering, and with the user's own voice absent from the downlink apart from faint sidetone. **No-go for the accessory** if fewer than 2 of 3 phones pass, or if any pass requires manual route selection on most calls; then switch investment to the conference fallback and stop all accessory UI work. No caption UI is built for calls until Gate 0 passes.

**Gate 0b, same-phone mic (Stage 1 side test).** If `isClientSilenced()` is true or capture RMS is within 3 dB of digital silence during SIM calls on all tested phones, the same-phone route is formally closed and never revisited without a platform change.

**Gate 0c, conference fallback (Stage 1 side test). Go** as fallback if merges succeed in at least 9 of 10 attempts on Jazz and at least one other operator's prepaid SIM, the cloud leg receives the remote caller's audio for at least 99% of merged time, bridge latency adds less than 400 ms p95, and a legitimate in-country number can be obtained in writing.

**Gate 1, transport robustness (Stage 3–4).** Frame loss below 1% and no gap longer than 500 ms over 30-minute calls; reconnect within 3 s after a Bluetooth drop; works on at least 10 of 12 Stage 4 phones (at least 80% of the market by brand weight).

**Gate 2, caption quality (Stage 2 and 6).** On the narrowband benchmark with script-normalised scoring, the chosen cloud engine reaches WER at or below 25% for English, 30% for Urdu and 35% for code-switched speech; the on-device engine reaches at or below 40% on Urdu (otherwise on-device stays a no-data fallback only, not the default); duplicated finals occur in zero test utterances; and at least 8 of 10 Deaf or hard-of-hearing testers rate a typical call as understandable (4 or 5 on a 5-point scale).

**Gate 3, latency (Stage 5).** First partial at or below 1.0 s p50 and 1.5 s p95 from audio onset; final at or below 2.0 s p50 after the end of speech; on-device and cloud measured separately.

**Gate 4, cost, battery and compliance (Stage 7).** Phone battery drain at or below 10% per hour of captioned call on a 5,000 mAh budget phone; accessory runtime at least 4 hours of talk (wireless form); data use at or below 30 MB per hour in cloud mode and zero in on-device mode; a retail accessory price the team and its NGO partners judge affordable (target to be set after BOM costing, not assumed here); written legal advice on consent; Bluetooth qualification and PTA type-approval paths confirmed. Failing Gate 4 on price alone redirects toward NGO-subsidised distribution, not toward the policy-blocked same-phone route.
