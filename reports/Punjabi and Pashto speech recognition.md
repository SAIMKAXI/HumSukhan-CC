# Ship Pashto now, build Shahmukhi Punjabi deliberately

HumSukhan should treat its two new languages on different timelines. **Pashto can probably reach users within weeks.** Deepgram nova-3, the engine HumSukhan already streams through, added Pashto (`ps`, `ps-AF`) for batch and streaming on 27 Aug 2026 ([Deepgram changelog, snippet](https://developers.deepgram.com/changelog/2026/8/27)). Commercially clean open data now exists to build an offline Pashto model: Common Voice Pashto (CC0) holds roughly **960–976 validated hours in CV24** ([Rahman, arXiv 2604.06507](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.06507)). A Whisper-base fine-tune reaches **27.1% WER / 8.8% CER** on that data and slots into the existing sherpa-onnx Whisper-int8 path, though that figure is author-reported and measured on read speech. **Pakistani Punjabi has no shippable solution today.** No cloud vendor documents Shahmukhi output, and every "Punjabi" offering is Indian Gurmukhi (`pa-IN`). Even Meta's Omnilingual ASR, the only large open model trained on `pnb_Arab`, reports **31.2% CER on Shahmukhi from only 11.9 training hours** ([Omnilingual per-language results](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr/main/per_language_results_table_7B_llm_asr.csv)). The recommended approach has three parts. First, cloud-first live captions for both languages: Deepgram `ps` directly, and Deepgram/Soniox `pa` passed through a licence-clean Gurmukhi→Shahmukhi transliterator as a stop-gap. Second, offline models that HumSukhan retrains itself from MIT/Apache bases on CC0/CC-BY data, with documented data lineage. Third, a long-term streaming Zipformer for each region (Urdu+Pashto+English, Urdu+Punjabi+English), distilled from those teachers. That model is the only design that meets the sub-second-partial, 2–4 GB RAM and code-switching requirements together. For Punjabi, the real bottleneck is 50–100 hours of consented, transcribed Pakistani Shahmukhi speech, which HumSukhan must license, partner for, or collect. Model choice matters far less than that data.

*Evidence labels used below. **[Verified]** means a resource was confirmed to exist on 26 Oct 2026 by reading its Hugging Face model or dataset card or file listing through the Hugging Face API, or by reading the raw file on GitHub. **[Snippet]** means the cited page could not be loaded in the research sandbox, so the fact comes from a search-engine snippet of that page and must be re-checked before it drives a decision. This applies to most vendor docs, arxiv.org, aclanthology.org, LDC, Appen and the Mozilla Data Collective. **Author-reported** means the number comes from the model card or paper's own authors. **Independent** means a third party re-ran the evaluation. **[Inference]** marks our own reasoning. None of the numbers in this report was reproduced by HumSukhan.*

## Pashto has enough open data, but off-the-shelf models write the wrong script

The defining fact about Pashto ASR is that general-purpose models fail at the script level before they fail at the word level. In the first reproducible multi-model benchmark, every Whisper size scored **90–297% WER** on FLEURS and Common Voice Pashto. **No Whisper model produced Pashto script in more than 0.8% of utterances**; it wrote Arabic, Dari or Urdu spellings instead. By contrast, MMS-1B, SeamlessM4T and Omnilingual ASR each exceeded **93% Pashto-script fidelity** [Verified, abstract read in full] ([Rahman, arXiv 2604.04598](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.04598)). This explains why HumSukhan's current Whisper-based Urdu path cannot simply be pointed at Pashto. The benchmark also found that Pashto-specific sounds (the retroflex series and the lateral fricatives) carry a disproportionate share of errors.

Fine-tuning fixes most of the script problem cheaply. A 2026 study on Common Voice Pashto compared adaptation strategies on whisper-base. **Vanilla full fine-tuning reached 21.22% WER / 8.52% CER**, freezing two encoder layers gave 35.98%, LoRA r=64 gave 54.58%, and an Urdu→Pashto staged transfer gave 65.78%. **Urdu transfer actively hurt**, and the authors suggest Persian/Dari as a better intermediate language ([arXiv 2604.06507](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.06507)). At 113 hours of CV24 training data the scale curve is shallow: whisper-base 27.13% WER, whisper-small 24.89%, large-v3-turbo 23.37%, with CER of 8.79%, 8.08% and 7.64%. The authors call whisper-small "the practical optimum" ([arXiv 2604.06507](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.06507)). For HumSukhan the more useful reading is that **whisper-base gives up only about 2 WER points to a model three times its size**, which makes it the natural on-device candidate. The same paper found that online speed and noise augmentation was worth **7.25 WER points** (27.13% vs 34.38%). Its dominant remaining errors are the masculine ی versus feminine ه word-final suffix and the consonant څ.

Those headline numbers come from read speech in the same domain as training, and real captioning will be harder. In the cross-domain benchmark, community fine-tunes that publish about 14% WER **degrade to 32.5–59% WER** on out-of-distribution test sets ([arXiv 2604.04598](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.04598)). A large-v3-turbo fine-tune trained on 395 hours scores **17.85% WER on read CV27 but 41.04% on Spontaneous Pashto Speech** [Verified card, author-reported] ([nassimjp/whisper-v1-pashto-turbo](https://huggingface.co/nassimjp/whisper-v1-pashto-turbo)). HumSukhan should therefore plan for live conversational WER in the 35–45% range from first-generation models. Pashto's rich morphology inflates WER relative to readability, so CER is the better guide [Inference].

Dialect coverage is the main unknown. Pashto has two major dialect groups. Northern (Yusufzai/Peshawar) and Southern (Kandahari/Quetta) differ in vowels, some consonant correspondences and vocabulary, yet **no dialect-stratified WER has been published for any model**. Common Voice captures both groups, but the authors describe the distribution as "uncontrolled and unbalanced" and the speaker metadata as "inconsistently labelled" ([arXiv 2604.06507](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.06507)). Every cloud vendor labels Pashto as Afghanistan (`ps-AF`), and FLEURS is `ps_af` only [Verified] ([google/fleurs](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/fleurs)). The best open signal of Pakistani coverage is Meta's Omnilingual ASR. It reports separate Northern (`pbu_Arab`, CER 7.5 from 5.2 h), Central (`pst_Arab`, CER 3.5) and Southern (`pbt_Arab`, CER 8.8) results for its 7B model [Verified, author-reported] ([results CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr/main/per_language_results_table_7B_llm_asr.csv)), and its corpus photos show collection "in Pakistan and Liberia" ([sherpa-onnx Omnilingual card](https://huggingface.co/csukuangfj/sherpa-onnx-omnilingual-asr-1600-languages-300M-ctc-int8-2025-11-12)). Two open models claim Pakistani dialects. **Katib-ASR** from the University of Peshawar claims "Afghan & Pakistani dialects" at 28.23% WER on its own test set, but its card declares **no licence** ([uzair0/Katib-ASR](https://huggingface.co/uzair0/Katib-ASR)). The nassimjp turbo model's card explicitly warns against commercial use without a rights review. Neither can ship as-is. Both authors are worth contacting.

## Pakistani Punjabi is the harder problem, and its sister varieties are harder still

For Shahmukhi Punjabi, the script failure seen with Pashto is built into the tooling itself. Whisper's `pa` token means Eastern Punjabi in Gurmukhi. The one documented Shahmukhi fine-tune works around this by forcing the **Urdu token (`ur`)** [Verified card] ([Ghost3454/translynx-pakistani-punjabi-whisper-small](https://huggingface.co/Ghost3454/translynx-pakistani-punjabi-whisper-small)). CLE/UET Lahore researchers found that Whisper "defaulted to Gurmukhi" on Pakistani Punjabi broadcast vlogs. Zero-shot Whisper-large scored about **54.7% WER**, and fine-tuned whisper-small improved only from about **45.2% WER with 1 h to 38.0% with 15 h** of Majhi-dialect data [Snippet] ([CHiPSAL 2025, ACL 2025.chipsal-1.20](https://aclanthology.org/2025.chipsal-1.20/)). MMS-1B-all ships a Gurmukhi `pan` adapter but **no `pnb`, `skr` or `hno` adapter** [Verified file listing] ([facebook/mms-1b-all](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b-all)). SeamlessM4T-v2 likewise has `pa` but no `pnb` ([facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large](https://huggingface.co/facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large)). Both are CC-BY-NC anyway.

Meta's Omnilingual ASR (Apache-2.0) is the exception. Its language list contains `pnb_Arab`, `skr_Arab` (Saraiki), `hno_Arab` (Hindko) and `phr_Arab` (Pahari-Pothwari), alongside `pan_Guru` and three Urdu scripts [Verified] ([lang_ids.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr/main/src/omnilingual_asr/models/wav2vec2_llama/lang_ids.py)). Its own 7B results show how thin the Shahmukhi coverage is. **pnb_Arab scored 31.2 CER from 11.9 training hours, while pan_Guru scored 4.3 from 207.5 h.** Saraiki scored 17.0 CER from 1.5 h, Hindko 17.1 from 12.2 h, and Pothwari 23.0 from 17.4 h ([results CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr/main/per_language_results_table_7B_llm_asr.csv)). These are figures for a 7.8B server model; the 300M CTC model that fits a phone has **no published pnb CER** and will be worse [Inference]. That pnb scores worse than its lower-resource sisters probably reflects messy transcripts rather than acoustics. A preview of the Omnilingual `pnb_Arab` training transcripts shows East-Punjabi/Hindi vocabulary rendered in Shahmukhi, such as مکھ (mukh, "main") and پرکار (prakar, "type"), mixed with Urdu spellings like سکتے ہیں [Verified preview] ([facebook/omnilingual-asr-corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/omnilingual-asr-corpus)). The pnb CER is therefore partly a property of the transcripts [Inference].

A Gurmukhi-ASR-plus-transliteration pipeline is technically credible but has real limits. Gurmukhi→Shahmukhi transliteration is close to solved at the text level. The SLPG fairseq model reports **99.5% word accuracy (BLEU 98.1)**, against BLEU 87.7 for the reverse direction [Verified card, author-reported] ([SLPG G→S model](https://huggingface.co/SLPG/Punjabi_Gurmukhi_to_Shahmukhi_Transliteration)), and Lehal's system reports above 98.6% ([Lehal, learnpunjabi.org](https://learnpunjabi.org/pdf/GurmukhiToShahmukhiTransliteration.pdf)). A LUMS Interspeech 2026 analysis of Urdu, Punjabi, Pashto and Sindhi found three things: script mismatch is most of Whisper's error in three of the four languages, **transliteration post-processing recovers up to 25 WER points**, and spelling and Unicode variation alone inflates WER by up to 3.1 points [Snippet] ([Azeemi, Qazi, Mustafa, Raza, Interspeech 2026](https://aghaaliraza.com/publications/2026--Interspeech--Dissecting_ASR_Failures_in_Low-Resource_South_Asian_Languages.pdf)). The limits are lexical and legal. Transliteration keeps Indian word choice: a Sanskritised ਪ੍ਰਕਾਰ becomes پرکار where a Lahori speaker would write قسم. It also has to guess etymological letters (س/ص/ث, ز/ذ/ض/ظ) for loanwords. Gurmukhi models trained on Indian speakers will also mis-hear Pakistani accents and Urdu code-switches, and the errors compound [Inference]. Finally, **neither the SLPG models nor their 6.3M-pair corpus declares a licence** [Verified card] ([SLPG corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SLPG/Punjabi_Transliteration_Corpus)). The most mature transliterator therefore cannot ship until its licence is clarified.

Saraiki, Hindko and Pothwari belong in the Punjabi programme as related varieties, but not on the first release. Each has a small CC0 Common Voice set: Saraiki 4.33 validated hours from 60 speakers ([CV 24.0 Saraiki, snippet](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmj8u3pta00phnxxbsw61jb2r)), Pahari-Pothwari 14.3 h ([CV 24.0, snippet](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmj8u3pmm00l5nxxbcnnigee7)) and Northern Hindko 10.15 h, whose datasheet forbids re-hosting ([CV 25.0, snippet](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmn2alr7p018jo1071gyxr951)). No Hindko or Pothwari ASR model exists on the Hub. A Saraiki Whisper repo that appeared in search results (`themohal/saraiki-whisper-medium`) returned "Repository not found" on 26 Oct 2026. That is enough data to add these varieties as dialect augmentation in a joint Perso-Arabic model and to measure Omnilingual zero-shot. It is not enough to advertise support. Saraiki's implosive letters (ٻ ڄ ݙ ڳ) must survive normalisation, and no open transliterator handles them.

**Text data for language models and normalisation** includes the following. FineWeb-2 `pbt_Arab` has one 855.7 MB shard [Verified], with its ODC-By licence not re-checked. Pashto Wikipedia is 53.3 MB (CC-BY-SA). Punjabi Wikipedia `pnb` has 72.3K articles and FineWeb-2 `pnb_Arab` has 20.8K documents, the latter heavily overlapping Wikipedia ([FineWeb-2](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-2)). Several Shahmukhi literature corpora on the Mozilla Data Collective are **CC-BY-NC** and so unusable for a commercial LM [Snippet] ([MDC Mediamen](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/organization/cmhq65prc00mqnz07ox7oejap)).

The asymmetry is stark. For Pashto, the binding constraint is not volume. Most published models used only the roughly 94-hour official CV train split, out of a validated pool ten to thirty times larger ([arXiv 2604.06507](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.06507)). The real gaps are the absence of Pakistani-dialect labels and the scarcity of conversational speech. For Shahmukhi, about 7 hours of open, commercially usable audio exists in total. Common Voice has **no `pnb` locale**; Punjabi there is `pa-IN` only ([MDC listings, snippet](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets?page=4)). Data must come from licensing (Appen's 20 h), a partnership (CLE/KICS), sponsoring a Common Voice `pnb` locale (CC0 sentences and recordings through the standard Pontoon and Sentence Collector workflow, [snippet](https://redirect.github.com/common-voice/common-voice/blob/main/docs/LANGUAGE.md)), or paid collection. Andovar, for instance, advertises Shahmukhi-speaker collection ([snippet](https://andovar.com/languages/punjabi-data-services-for-ai/)). Pashto's own history shows that media partnerships move these numbers. Common Voice Pashto contributors grew roughly **108-fold** between CV17 and CV18 alongside a VOA Pashto broadcast campaign [Snippet] ([arXiv 2603.27021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27021)).

## The techniques that matter: script normalisation, joint Urdu training, distillation into a streaming model

**Fine-tuning strategy.** Use full fine-tuning for any model under about 250M parameters. LoRA under-adapted badly on whisper-base Pashto (54.58% vs 21.22% WER), although the authors stress that their LoRA setup was untuned ([arXiv 2604.06507](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.06507)). Below about one hour of data, adapter methods like MMS win. Above one or two hours, XLS-R-class models reach parity ([FieldMatters 2025, snippet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17459)), so the MMS adapter recipe's advantage disappears at the data scales HumSukhan will work with. Its non-commercial licence removes it anyway. For Pashto, build custom speaker-disjoint, sentence-deduplicated splits from the full CC0 validated pool. Add Spontaneous Speech, FLEURS and the Omnilingual pbu/pbt/pst splits, apply online speed and noise augmentation, and add 8 kHz narrowband simulation if call captioning is in scope. For Punjabi, the most promising hypothesis is **joint training of Urdu and Shahmukhi in one Perso-Arabic vocabulary** [Inference]. Shahmukhi uses the Urdu alphabet plus extra letters ([Wikipedia: Shahmukhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahmukhi)). Pakistani Punjabi borrows heavily from Urdu, and the TransLynx model shows the Whisper `ur` token can carry Shahmukhi. The Pashto study's finding that staged Urdu→Pashto transfer hurt is a warning, but Punjabi is much closer to Urdu than Pashto is. HumSukhan should test that difference directly rather than assume it.

**Pseudo-labelling and distillation.** The large models give HumSukhan server-side teachers that are too big for phones. Candidates include a fine-tuned large-v3-turbo, BuzzASR (MIT), and Omnilingual LLM-7B (Apache-2.0, about 17 GiB VRAM). Omnilingual's zero-shot model with 10 in-context examples reportedly cut average CER on unseen languages from 26.3% to 14.4% [secondary summary] ([Emergent Mind on arXiv 2511.09690](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2511.09690)). That makes it a plausible pnb pseudo-labeller. A teacher can label hundreds of hours of unlabelled audio, filtered by confidence or by agreement between two models, to train a small student. Two licensing rules apply. Teachers must be commercially licensed, so **pseudo-labels from CC-BY-NC models such as MMS or Seamless are a legal grey area to avoid**. Cloud vendor terms on training with their outputs must be checked before using Deepgram transcripts this way. Possible sources of unlabelled audio are YODAS2 (YouTube CC), the Radio Pakistan Peshawar archive, and consented user "data donation". The archive holds about 10,000 digitised hours, including 400 Pashto dramas, with no licensing terms found [Snippet] ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1370926)).

**Transliteration and LM fusion.** Gurmukhi→Shahmukhi transliteration has two uses: an interim cloud bridge, and converting Gurmukhi speech corpora (Kathbath, FLEURS, CV pa-IN) into extra Shahmukhi training targets. Neither use has a published evaluation, and the second risks teaching the model Indian lexicon [Inference]. Byte-level transliterators (ByT5 class) beat subword models in a comparable Tajik–Farsi benchmark ([arXiv 2605.02270, snippet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02270)). That is a reasonable architecture if HumSukhan must train its own licence-clean transliterator. For CTC models, an n-gram LM built from licence-clean Wikipedia and FineWeb-2 text plus sherpa-onnx hotwords for names is cheap. Its effect on Pashto and Punjabi is **unmeasured** and needs testing.

**Code-switching and language ID.** No Pashto–Urdu, Pashto–English or Punjabi–Urdu code-switching ASR corpus or paper exists. The nearest is UrduSpeech, a 156 h corpus that includes Urdu–English switching ([arXiv 2605.17846, snippet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17846)). In the short term, keep HumSukhan's dual-socket arbitration and make the second language a user-selected profile: Urdu+English (today), Pashto+Urdu, or Punjabi+Urdu. The arbitration rules change per profile [Inference]. Pashto versus Urdu can be scored by Pashto-only code points (ټ ډ ړ ښ ږ څ ځ ګ ڼ ې ۍ) against Urdu-only ones (ٹ ڈ ڑ ے ہ ھ), plus function words (Pashto د، چې، کې، سره، ته، دی، ده versus Urdu کا، کی، کے، کو، میں، ہے). For Punjabi versus Urdu, Deepgram's Gurmukhi output makes the script itself the arbiter before transliteration. A Shahmukhi model needs function words instead (Punjabi دا، دی، دے، نوں، وچ، نال، سی، اے versus the Urdu set). The same lists, plus question words (Pashto څه، ولې، چېرته، کله، څوک، څنګه; Punjabi کیہ، کیوں، کتھے، کدوں، کون، کیویں), are what `turn_taking.dart` will need for each new language. These starter lists come from our linguistic knowledge, not the research notes, and must be validated by native speakers. For an on-device LID option, SpeechBrain's VoxLingua107 ECAPA is **Apache-2.0** and covers `pa`, `ps` and `ur` [Verified] ([card](https://hf.co/speechbrain/lang-id-voxlingua107-ecapa)). Its accuracy on short and Shahmukhi-speaker segments is unknown, and MMS-LID is non-commercial. The long-term answer to intra-sentence switching is one model per region with a shared vocabulary, which removes routing entirely.

**Streaming on-device architecture.** None of the large multilingual models streams. The proven sherpa-onnx route to true streaming is an icefall streaming Zipformer transducer. Small 20M-parameter English and Chinese models are documented as "suitable for Cortex A7 CPU". A bilingual small model's int8 encoder is 41 MB, and exports come at chunk sizes of 32, 64 or 96 frames ([sherpa docs, raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/k2-fsa/sherpa/master/docs/source/onnx/pretrained_models/online-transducer/zipformer-transducer-models.rst)). Low-resource community models such as the Bengali Vosk Zipformer packaged in Feb 2026 show that the path works for new languages (same source). The only RTF figures found are desktop numbers: a 71M Korean streaming Zipformer at **RTF 0.038 on one x86 thread** at int8 ([kangkyu card, snippet](https://huggingface.co/kangkyu/icefall-asr-ko-streaming-zipformer-72m)). **No measured RTF or RAM on Cortex-A53/A55 phones exists for any candidate**, so HumSukhan has to measure this itself. Until a streaming student exists, offline captions for new languages will follow the current Urdu pattern of VAD segments decoded per utterance. That pattern cannot deliver a first partial in under a second for Whisper. A CTC model re-decoding its growing buffer every 400–600 ms might, because CTC decoding is non-autoregressive, but it is 4–5× Whisper-base's parameter count. This must be benchmarked [Inference].

## Recommendation: cloud now, in-house offline models next, one streaming model per region later

The recommended approach is **cloud-first for live captions, with retrained open models for offline use**, and the two languages on different tracks. It balances quality, effort and maintainability better than the alternatives. Building from scratch is never necessary, because every candidate base is pretrained. Waiting for a vendor to ship Shahmukhi has no visible end date.

For **Pashto**, run Deepgram nova-3 `ps` as a second socket beside `ur` behind a "Pashto + Urdu" profile. If it passes the gates below, release it as a beta quickly: the integration cost is a language parameter and an arbitration rule set. At about $0.29 per socket-hour, two sockets cost roughly **$0.58 per captioned hour**. That price comes from a third-party rate, so verify it on Deepgram's own pricing page. In parallel, retrain whisper-base, and whisper-small if the phone allows, from OpenAI's MIT checkpoints on CC0/CC-BY data only. Export them to sherpa-onnx Whisper int8 to drop into the existing offline isolate, and fine-tune Omnilingual CTC-300M on the same data as the challenger. The reason to retrain rather than ship `ihanif/exp_*` is lineage, not quality. The recipe is public and runs on one 24 GB GPU in about 10k steps ([arXiv 2604.06507](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.06507)). Owning the checkpoint lets HumSukhan document every training clip's licence and keep improving the model when new data arrives.

For **Pakistani Punjabi**, accept that the first release will be an interim. Run Deepgram `pa` (and test Soniox `pa` and ElevenLabs Realtime `pa`) and transliterate the Gurmukhi output to Shahmukhi server-side, in a Supabase Edge Function or on the device. Only do this once a transliterator with a clear licence exists: license SLPG's or Lehal's system, or train a ByT5-style model on licence-clean parallel text. Mark the feature as beta, because the vocabulary will skew Indian. Meanwhile, invest in the real asset: **50–100 hours of consented, Shahmukhi-transcribed Pakistani speech**. Collect it on low-end Android microphones across Majhi/Lahori, Saraiki-belt and Pothwari speakers, including natural Urdu and English switching. Then fine-tune a joint Urdu+Shahmukhi model on it, choosing between Whisper with the `ur` token and Omnilingual CTC-300M by experiment. A Shahmukhi model that also handles Urdu natively would replace the dual-socket arbitration for Punjab users altogether.

For the **end state**, train one streaming Zipformer per region (Urdu+Pashto+English, Urdu+Punjabi+English). Use 20–70M parameters, int8, at about 50–90 MB, and distil it from the fine-tuned teachers on several hundred pseudo-labelled hours. This is the only architecture in the evidence that plausibly meets sub-second partials, 2 GB phones and intra-sentence code-switching together. It also collapses HumSukhan's offline stack onto the sherpa-onnx OnlineRecognizer it already uses for English, which simplifies long-term maintenance. It is also the most expensive and least proven step. Zipformer research runs used up to 30 epochs on 8× V100 ([arXiv 2506.14434, snippet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14434)). It should wait until the teacher models and data exist.

The strongest alternative is "Omnilingual everywhere": one Apache-2.0 365 MB CTC model fine-tuned for all regional languages, decoded in VAD chunks. It has real appeal, because it means one runtime, many languages, correct scripts and Pakistani-collected training data. It loses on two counts. It does not stream, and its footprint is about 5× whisper-base on phones where HumSukhan already holds English and Urdu models in memory. If the device benchmark (E7 below) shows acceptable RTF and RAM on a 2–3 GB phone, it should replace Whisper as the offline Pashto and Punjabi model. The plan is built to let that result decide. A second alternative is to caption Punjabi speech directly in Urdu, via speech translation, for Deaf users schooled in Urdu. It is worth raising with Deaf testers as a product question, but it conflicts with the stated Shahmukhi requirement, and the only strong open model for it (Seamless) is non-commercial.

## Risks and unknowns the first experiments must retire

The largest unknown is **vendor accuracy on Pakistani speech**. No independent benchmark of Deepgram nova-3, Soniox, ElevenLabs, Azure or Google exists for Pashto or Pakistani Punjabi. Everything about their script output and accent robustness comes from marketing pages read via search snippets. Deepgram's own Punjabi page says Gurmukhi, and its predecessor nova-2 scored 100% WER on FLEURS Pashto in a competitor's table. The second is **dialect generalisation**. No Pashto or Punjabi dialect-stratified result exists anywhere, and every Pashto cloud locale is Afghan. The third is **device performance**. No source measured RTF, RAM or latency for any candidate on Cortex-A53/A55 hardware. The memory question is acute on 2 GB phones, which may need to unload the Urdu Whisper model before loading a 365 MB Omnilingual model [Inference].

Licensing risks are concrete and checkable. The SLPG transliterator and corpus have no licence. Katib-ASR has no licence, and nassimjp's card disclaims commercial rights. The sherpa-onnx Omnilingual export card shows no licence tag, though upstream is Apache-2.0. A third-party CoreML conversion of the same model is labelled CC-BY-NC, which shows why **every converted artefact needs its own licence check** ([snippet](https://huggingface.co/aoiandroid/omni-asr-coreml-int8)). Babel's commercial terms through LDC are unknown. CC-BY-4.0 sources (FLEURS, the Omnilingual corpus) require attribution in the app's notices. The Common Voice Hindko datasheet forbids re-hosting.

Legal and ethical risk sits in data collection. Pakistan has **no enacted standalone data-protection law** that could be verified; PECA 2016, as amended in 2025, is the operative statute ([DLA Piper, snippet](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=PK)). One unverified guide claims that recording conversations without every participant's consent is a criminal offence ([recordinglaw.com, snippet](https://recordinglaw.com/pakistan-recording-laws)). Captioning captures third parties' voices, so any audio retained for training needs an explicit, separate "data donation" opt-in with consent materials in Urdu, Pashto and Shahmukhi. Vendor-side training should be disabled with `mip_opt_out=true` on Deepgram ([Deepgram MIP docs, snippet](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/the-deepgram-model-improvement-partnership-program)). Font coverage is not a blocker: we checked HumSukhan's bundled `NotoNastaliqUrdu-Regular.ttf` (app repository, `fonts/noto_sans/`) with fontTools on 2026-10-26, and its character map contains every Pashto-specific letter (ټ ډ ړ ښ ږ ځ څ ې ۍ ئ ڼ), the Saraiki implosives (ٻ ڄ ݙ ڳ ݨ) and the Shahmukhi letters (ڑ ٹ ڈ ں ے ۓ ھ). Pashto is conventionally set in Naskh rather than Nastaliq, so a Naskh face (for example Noto Naskh Arabic) is a readability choice to test with Pashto readers, not a correctness requirement.

## A staged starting plan with go/no-go gates

### Evaluation protocol: script-correct CER first

Every experiment uses one scoring harness. **Tier A normalisation** is always applied, to both reference and hypothesis:

- Unicode NFC.
- Strip diacritics (U+064B–U+065F, U+0670) and tatweel (U+0640).
- Unify ZWNJ/ZWJ handling.
- Map Arabic and Extended Arabic-Indic digits to ASCII.
- Strip punctuation (، ۔ ؟ . , ?).
- Map code-point variants that readers treat as the same letter: Arabic ك→ک, and Arabic ي/ى→ی. For Pashto, keep ې, ۍ and ئ distinct. For Urdu and Shahmukhi, map ه→ہ outside ھ, keep ے distinct, and keep ں and the Saraiki implosives distinct.
- For Pashto, apply Katib-ASR's mapping of گ and ݢ→ګ ([Katib card](https://huggingface.co/uzair0/Katib-ASR)).
- **Never** collapse the Pashto ی/ه gender suffix, which is a real grammatical error.

**Tier B normalisation** maps cross-language leakage onto the target script: Urdu ٹ→Pashto ټ, ہ→ه, and so on. It is used only to compute a "lenient" CER that shows how much error is purely orthographic.

**Primary metric:** Tier-A CER. **Secondary:** Tier-A WER, and English segments scored in Latin script. For a curated list of nativised loans, accept either Latin or the established Urdu spelling, matching HumSukhan's existing caption convention.

**Script fidelity** is the share of utterances in which at least 95% of letters belong to the target inventory. A **leakage rate** counts Urdu-only letters in Pashto output, and Gurmukhi or Devanagari code points (U+0A00–U+0A7F, U+0900–U+097F) in Shahmukhi output. Any Gurmukhi output is a hard failure.

**Latency** is measured from speech onset to the first partial (p50/p90) and from end of speech to the final text. **Device metrics** are RTF, peak RSS, model file size, and battery drain per captioned hour. A **Deaf-user comprehension check** asks testers to answer content questions from captions, because WER does not measure usefulness.

**Anchor every threshold to Urdu.** Score HumSukhan's current Deepgram `ur` and Whisper-base-int8 Urdu on an Urdu set recorded the same way. That measures the level users already accept.

### Partners and data to pursue

On the research side, three groups stand out. **LUMS** (Agha Ali Raza, Ihsan Ayyub Qazi) has already analysed ASR failures across Urdu, Punjabi, Pashto and Sindhi and is the natural evaluation partner. **CLE/KICS at UET Lahore** holds the non-public Bhulekha Shahmukhi ASR set, a Shahmukhi TTS corpus and a phonetic lexicon ([ISCA archive, snippet](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/naseem25_interspeech.html)). Pashto ASR expertise is concentrated in a few people: **Hanif Rahman** (ihanif), author of most 2026 Pashto ASR papers and models; **Muhammad Uzair at the University of Peshawar**, author of Katib-ASR, whose licence could be negotiated; and UET Peshawar's speech group. For data, the priorities are **Appen** (PAP_ASR001, the only off-the-shelf Pakistani Punjabi set, and its Pashto telephone sets), **LDC** (Babel Pashto commercial terms), **Mozilla Common Voice** (a `pnb` locale, with past in-country grants of up to $50K per project, [snippet](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/common-voice/in-country-programmes/)), and **Radio Pakistan Peshawar** or regional FM stations for archives and contributor drives. ConnectHear and DeafTawk are better suited as co-design and user-testing partners than as data sources. ConnectHear already has GSMA Innovation Fund backing in Pakistan ([GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/digital-grantees-portfolio/connecthear/)), and GSMA, Lacuna Fund and Ignite's planned AI fund are plausible co-funders for the Shahmukhi collection.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question HumSukhan should be asking. "Which model supports Punjabi and Pashto?" turns out to have a misleading answer: many claim to, and almost none write the script Pakistani users read. Script fidelity, not WER, is the first filter. HumSukhan's evaluation harness, with its strict normalisation and Gurmukhi and Urdu leakage counters, is therefore as strategic an asset as any model. Pashto has crossed a data threshold in the past eighteen months. It is now mainly an engineering problem of dialect validation and on-device packaging. Shahmukhi Punjabi is still a data problem, and any vendor or model that skips that step will produce captions in Indian vocabulary or Urdu spelling.

The cheapest high-value move is also the fastest: E0–E2 cost a few engineer-weeks and almost no money, and they decide whether Pashto ships this quarter. The highest-leverage long-term move is a hypothesis no one has tested yet. If one model trained jointly on Urdu and Shahmukhi can serve Punjab users, it replaces two-socket arbitration with native code-switching and turns HumSukhan's existing Urdu investment into a Punjabi head start. If HumSukhan validates it, it would be among the first teams to ship Shahmukhi speech recognition at all. That position is worth more than a marginal WER gain on any public benchmark.
